# Bioinformatician — Interview Questions

A curated collection of interview questions (with answers) for **Bioinformatician** roles — one of the most established and broadly-scoped titles in computational biology, spanning academic research labs, biotech and pharma companies, genomics core facilities, clinical diagnostic labs, and public health institutions.

"Bioinformatician" covers genuinely wide territory, and different roles under this title can look quite different in practice — a bioinformatician supporting a genomics core facility's sequencing pipeline does meaningfully different day-to-day work than one doing comparative phylogenetics in an academic lab, or one building production-grade variant-calling infrastructure at a clinical diagnostics company. This repo focuses on the broad, common core that essentially any bioinformatics role draws on — molecular biology fundamentals, sequence analysis algorithms, NGS/genomics pipelines, statistics, databases, and software/pipeline engineering practice — with additional categories covering commonly-encountered specializations (structural bioinformatics, phylogenetics, single-cell and multi-omics) that a well-rounded bioinformatician should have at least working familiarity with even outside their own primary specialty.

> **Disclaimer:** This is a community knowledge base, not a certification or official curriculum. Answers aim to be technically sound and interview-appropriate — concise, structured, demonstrating reasoning — not exhaustive textbook chapters. Tools, databases, and best practices in this field evolve quickly (a specific tool version or database's current status can change), so always verify current documentation and recent literature for anything used in real practice.

## 🧭 How to use this repo

- **Candidates:** Work through categories in order — molecular biology fundamentals (1) and sequence analysis algorithms (2) underpin everything else. Categories 8–9 cover specializations that not every role requires deep expertise in, but that most interviewers expect at least solid conceptual familiarity with, since bioinformatics roles frequently involve collaborating across these adjacent subfields even when they're not your primary focus.
- **Interviewers:** Pick 1–2 questions per category rather than exhausting every category in one loop, and weight categories according to the specific role — a pipeline-engineering-heavy role should lean on categories 3 and 7, while a more research-analysis-focused role should lean on categories 2, 4, 5, and 9. Category 10 (system design/case studies) is where practical engineering judgment under real-world constraints (messy data, ambiguous requirements, reproducibility) gets tested most directly.
- **Everyone:** Answers are written to show *reasoning* — why a specific algorithm's assumptions matter, why a particular pipeline design choice affects reproducibility or scalability — not just to state facts.
